# True water depth pass (`?art=lush-live`, water on by default)

Continues from the full-frame live 3D proof (`fac69fc`). No geometry, placement, camera or composition change. No fog, particles, caustics, bloom, post-processing or new assets.

## Coordinate model (verified from the GLB)

The approved camera is at Three `(0, 6.6, 15.3)`, outside the tank. The front glass is the plane z = +5 (export metadata `front_glass_z_three`), and the rear boundary is at z ≈ −4.8. The water path is **only the glass-to-fragment part of the view ray**:

```
d = |P − C| · clamp((glassZ − P.z) / (C.z − P.z), 0, 1)     (0 for P.z ≥ glassZ)
```

This is `waterPathLength` in `habitat/shaders/water-volume.js`, mirrored exactly in GLSL. The air between camera and glass is not counted.

## Model

- **Extinction:** per channel, `T = exp(−σ · max(0, d − d_ref))`.
  - `σ = [0.13, 0.08, 0.058]` per scene unit, so red is lost fastest and blue least.
  - `d_ref = 3.5` units, the documented compensation (see below).
- **In-scattering:** analytic, `(1 − T) · scatterColor · mix(depthLight, 1, h)`.
  - `scatterColor` (linear, pre-tone-mapping) is `[0.058, 0.112, 0.118]`, a restrained blue-green at the scene's own radiance level.
  - `h` is the normalised mean height of the water path between the floor (y = 0.1) and the surface (y = 7.2).
  - `depthLight = 0.5`: the lit water column is a little weaker toward the floor.
- **Composition of the result:** `outgoingLight · T + in-scatter`, in linear light before tone mapping, on the real materials. That covers all 32 environment materials (alpha test, double-sidedness, depth test/write and glTF maps are untouched), the rear boundary and the Hero Fish.
- **Compensation (bounded, documented):** the foreground reference distance `d_ref = 3.5` is a per-channel white balance equal to the transmittance of 3.5 units of water. It is placed just in front of the near planting. Nothing is ever brightened (`max(0, …)`).
  - **Why it's needed:** without it, the 4+ units of water in front of the near plants tinted the foreground as strongly as a colour grade, and the fresh greens and pale sand went dull.
  - **Lighting:** the `fac69fc` lighting and exposure are unchanged.
- **Per-vertex evaluation:** the model is evaluated per vertex and interpolated, since vegetation and fish triangles are small. The rear boundary (12 large triangles) is evaluated exactly per fragment. The measured reason is under Performance.
- **Development switch:** `?water=off|extinction|full`, default `full`. It is read in `habitat.js` only; there is no UI.

The first implementation evaluated the model per fragment. It cost about 1 FPS (58.6–58.8 mean, frame time 17.0–17.1 ms) because the scene is fragment-bound on overlapping alpha-tested foliage. Moving the evaluation to the vertex stage removed that regression with no visible difference (the rear-wall colour changed by ≤ 2/255).

## Assessment

- **Distance now reads as water at normal size, without labels.**
  - The foreground (near leaves, wood, gravel) is essentially unchanged.
  - The midground loses a little contrast.
  - The rear feather plants and ribbons clearly recede into blue-green water.
  - The rear boundary is no longer a dark card: it reads as the lit far water of the tank.
- **Extinction alone (B) is not enough.** It mostly darkens and makes the rear blacker, which is physically right but looks like a darker room. The in-scatter term (C) is what makes the rear belong to the water.
- **Weak points:**
  - The upper background is a fairly uniform grey-teal. In the upper right, far from plants, it can read slightly as haze rather than depth.
  - The overall image is still greener, greyer and darker than the bright `6dfd62d` tank: the sand is grey-beige and the water colour is duller than the approved clear blue. These are lighting and material issues, deliberately not addressed here.
  - The simplified glTF materials remain: large broad leaves look flat and some leaf backs stay dark. The water pass did not make them worse.
  - Fish are small at desktop distance, so the red accents read clearly only on front and mid fish.
