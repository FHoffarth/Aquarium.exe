# Live underwater runtime architecture spike

Status: feasibility study only. No renderer, art, dependency, or host change is part of this commit. `6dfd62d` is the art-direction reference, not an exact pixel lock. Experimental runtime commits `1a8e2dd` and `7807333` must not be merged.

## Decision

Build toward **A: a live 3D planted environment**, exported from the approved Blender source and rendered in the existing Three.js scene with the existing live fish. Prove the depth relationship with a small vertical slice before exporting the whole tank. **B: a hybrid** is an optimization option only if the important foreground, middle, and background vegetation, substrate, wood, fish, and rear boundary still have real positions and depth testing. Retire **C: static RGB plus depth** as the environment architecture. Keep the current plate only as a temporary comparison while the slice is evaluated.

The first visual requirement is agreement among parallax-free but spatially consistent cues: fish and leaves occlude each other in both directions, light falls through the same volume, and the camera sees more water before far objects than near ones. Additional effects cannot repair missing spatial relationships.

## Evidence and root cause

The current `lush` runtime renders `habitat/assets/lush/environment_albedo.webp` on one full-screen plane (`habitat/core/lush-environment.js`). That plane disables depth test and depth write and is drawn behind the live fish. Its pixels contain plants, gravel, wood, lighting, and water coloration already composited together. The later depth-map grade changes those pixels before runtime, but the depth values do not participate in the runtime depth buffer, occlusion, lighting, or fish movement. Every fish is therefore in front of every pictured leaf and rock. The uniform backdrop and independent live overlays reveal the image even when its local color grade is plausible. More cyan, fog, particles, caustics, or post-processing would leave this contradiction intact.

The supplied 12-second, 1440 × 936 `riverscape.mp4` shows fish moving through dark green planted masses, distinct near and far leaves, small visible substrate openings, and a rear that recedes without a conspicuous effect layer. This is a visual observation of the supplied video, separate from the code findings below. The reference is darker than the approved bright aquarium; the spatial relationships, not its exact color grade, are the useful model.

### Deskworlds: what actually makes depth

Riverbed's fish/vegetation occlusion is the closest visual precedent. Reefscape supplies the more explicit water-path model. Combine those concepts, not their effect budgets.

## Aquarium.exe feasibility audit

The approved scene is **not inherently a flat image**. The saved local `art/work/lush-reference/lush-water-reference.blend` contains separate 3D plant families, gravel, rocks, wood, rear geometry, camera, and lights. Read-only inspection in Blender 5.2.2 found 66 meshes and 386,723 triangles total. Excluding 20 posed fish meshes (42,540 triangles; retain the existing runtime fish instead) and 22 offline particle meshes (440 triangles), the environment has **24 meshes, 343,743 triangles, about 399,282 mesh vertices, 32 distinct used materials, and roughly 79 occupied material groups**. These are source-scene counts, not measured GLB draw calls or runtime GPU use.

| Approved source content | Feasibility |
| --- | --- |
| Ribbon, grass, feather, stem, bush, broad-leaf, and low foreground vegetation | Real triangulated meshes with different silhouettes; export geometry, transforms, material assignments, and UVs. The family meshes are already aggregated, so batching materials may matter more than instancing. Do not replace them with uniform cards. |
| Gravel, scattered stones, wood, rocks, rear surface | Separate meshes. Keep visible substrate and wood in 3D. The rear is a real plane but needs a dark, restrained material and the same water model. Existing Slice C GLB contains useful individual source assets, not the approved layout. |
| Five reusable texture images | Rock, wood, and leaf albedos already exist in the repository's asset work; texture reuse and packing are possible without new dependencies or asset research. Actual GPU texture memory must be measured after export. |
| Camera | Blender perspective camera at `(0, -15.3, 6.6)`, looking approximately at `(0, 1, 2.5)`, 48 mm lens on a 36 mm sensor, 1920 × 1080. In Three's Y-up coordinates this is approximately `(0, 6.6, 15.3)` looking at `(0, 2.5, -1)`, horizontal FOV about 41.1° (vertical about 23.8°). Preserve this framing in the slice, adjusting clipping planes to encompass the whole tank. Small depth placement changes are allowed. |
| Lighting and materials | Four Cycles area lights plus world light establish the bright planted hierarchy. Cycles noise, ramps, bump, view-distance mixes, and other shader nodes will not transfer faithfully as glTF PBR materials. Export geometry and bake or approximate selected color/roughness/alpha properties; reconstruct a restrained Three overhead/fill lighting rig. Exact Cycles pixel matching is neither promised nor required. |

Blender exports glTF in Y-up coordinates, preserving mesh transforms and camera geometry, but [its glTF exporter does not carry area or world lighting as equivalent runtime lights](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/addons/import_export/scene_gltf2.html). The repository's vendored `GLTFLoader` has handlers for `KHR_lights_punctual`, `EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing`, and `EXT_texture_webp`; this is a capability check, not a claim that the current approved scene uses those extensions. `habitat/core/assets.js` already parses GLB and verifies manifest assets, so no new loader dependency is needed. The source `.blend` under `art/work/` is Git-ignored; a future reproducible migration must track the shipped GLB/texture outputs and export recipe, and explicitly decide how the source scene is retained. This audit does not modify the source or manifest.

The current Three runtime already has a single scene and renderer (`habitat/habitats/planted-tank/scene.js`, `habitat/habitat.js`), 60 FPS scheduling and pause/visibility behavior (`habitat/core/engine.js`), live instanced Hero Fish bodies and fins (`habitat/core/hero-fish.js`), schooling in `habitat/habitats/planted-tank/config.js`, GLB loading, and material shader hooks (`habitat/shaders/material-effects.js`). These are reusable. The existing Slice C geometry renderer proves GLB integration, but its composition is rejected. Its world-z haze and particles are not a substitute for camera-path attenuation. The current fish swim in a much smaller coordinate box than the Blender scene; a deliberate simulation-to-scene transform/depth calibration is needed, while preserving fish geometry, appearance, schooling, host, and controls.

## Architecture comparison

| Choice | Visual and implementation assessment | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — full live 3D** | Highest chance of convincing interleaving and common depth/light treatment while retaining the approved species and substrate. Direct GLB is feasible. The unoptimized source has substantial geometry and many material groups, alpha overdraw, and Cycles-only shaders; 60 FPS cannot be assumed. Vegetation vertex motion requires matching depth/shadow behavior. | **Destination**, gated by the slice and measured optimization. |
| **B — selective hybrid** | Keep all near/mid/far occluding vegetation, fish, hardscape, gravel, and rear wall spatial. Simplify only distant static geometry or material variety after profiling. A depth-aware rear layer may work if it never intersects fish and shares water/shading treatment. Reduces draw/overdraw but can lose approved identity if used indiscriminately. | **Fallback optimization within A**, not a return to a full-screen plate. |
| **C — RGB + depth plate** | Cheap (current experiment about 5 draw calls and 47,962 triangles at about 60 FPS) but one view's baked surfaces cannot exchange occlusion or shadow with moving fish and leaves. Per-pixel depth used only for a grade cannot restore absent geometry. Even depth-aware compositing would need segmentation, disocclusion content, and per-layer lighting; it would be more fragile than exporting existing meshes. | **Retire** for runtime environment. Keep for reference comparison only. |

Geometry count alone is not the budget. The approved scene has many thin and overlapping leaves, which can cost more in fragment/alpha processing than its 344k environment triangles suggest. Material consolidation, cutout thresholds, double-sided leaves only where necessary, shared textures, and selectively reduced shadow casters are the first optimizations. Instancing helps repeated small pieces, but applying it blindly to already aggregated, irregular plant masses could change the composition. Preserve transforms first; profile before simplifying. Alpha-tested foliage should depth-write so fish can pass behind it. Alpha blending is reserved for genuinely translucent parts such as existing fins, with sorting limitations checked in the slice.

## Minimum viable freshwater water pipeline

1. **One live spatial scene.** Export a small approved-asset subset to GLB with its positions, UVs, and material classes. Runtime fish and environment use the same depth buffer. The rear is a dim neutral/dark blue-green wall behind the far plants, never a luminous cyan card.
2. **A finite water column.** Define the front glass plane in world coordinates (approximately Blender `y = -5`, or Three `z = +5`; calibrate from the actual tank bounds). The camera is outside it. For each opaque fragment and fish fragment, intersect the camera ray with that plane and use only the segment from the glass to the fragment as water distance. Do **not** attenuate the camera's air gap. Clamp for geometry outside the column.
3. **Shared, conservative RGB extinction.** In linear lighting space, use `T_rgb = exp(-sigma_rgb * d_water)` and `color = lit_surface * T_rgb + restrained_scatter_rgb`. Tune coefficients on the approved scale so front greenery and fish stay natural while rear foliage loses a little red/contrast. This is a physically inspired control, not an attempt to reproduce Reefscape's marine constants.
4. **Cheap in-scattering and overhead illumination.** Start with analytic `scatter_rgb ≈ S_rgb(height, topLight) * (1 - T_rgb)` on the same fragment, with a low-intensity blue-green source and smooth vertical light gradient. A common, very slow surface-light parameter may modulate top illumination after the spatial slice succeeds. This avoids an initial full-screen depth ray march and heavy post stack.
5. **Consistent materials.** Apply the same water function to opaque plants, gravel, wood, wall, and fish. The background receives more water path because it is farther away, not because a cyan overlay covers the frame. Transparent fins need compatible treatment without double attenuation. Color management and tone mapping remain the existing runtime's, with shader insertion before tone mapping.
6. **Secondary atmosphere.** Add only sparse actual 3D suspended motes and then minimal plant-tip motion or restrained caustics if the slice already reads as water. Neither is an acceptance crutch.

No generic `scene.fog` is required for the initial slice. Shadowed top light is useful if it can be achieved within budget; first use the existing directional/ambient setup and verify all scene elements respond coherently. Avoid a fullscreen scattering pass unless the cheaper analytic path demonstrably fails the visual gate.

## Next visual gate: one small runtime vertical slice

Extract spatial regions from the **existing** approved meshes, retaining their original vertices, UVs, material classes, and relative depths rather than hand-remodeling plants. Since several source meshes span large areas, the exporter must partition or crop triangles by region without leaving obviously cut silhouettes. Include one substrate opening, a small existing wood/rock accent, foreground low/broad leaves, middle ribbon/bush or stems, far feather/grass masses, and the rear wall. Use the approved camera/framing (empty parts of the frame can remain unbuilt for this proof), plus several **existing live** fish remapped into the tank's water coordinates. Force or observe fish trajectories at both sides of a plant layer.

The gate is a real 1920 × 1080 host capture or 30–60-second clip beside the approved `6dfd62d` frame and supplied Riverscape video, judged at normal desktop size. Pass only if foreground is crisp, rear vegetation naturally loses contrast, the rear recedes, and at least one fish is visibly behind leaves while another is in front. The viewer should see a planted aquarium with water depth **before** noticing particles or caustics. If it still reads as fish over a picture, stop and fix geometry/depth placement or common light treatment; do not add effects or migrate the full tank.

## Performance envelope and migration

Prior Slice C reporting measured about 93,960 triangles at around 60 FPS with roughly 76–77% GPU use on the target Intel UHD desktop; the experimental plate measured around 47,962 triangles and five draw calls at around 60 FPS. These measurements are different scenes and do not predict the approved 344k-triangle environment. Raw geometry could occupy tens of MiB after GPU attribute/index expansion; texture residency, leaf overdraw, shader permutations, and shadow redraw are more uncertain. A preliminary target of a few dozen, rather than 79+, material draws is an optimization hypothesis, **not** a fixed pass threshold. Measure 1920 × 1080 visible runtime FPS, GPU utilization/frame time, draw calls, triangles, texture memory where available, and pause idle on the actual host. Quality scaling may use the existing render scale and selective shadows/vegetation LOD, while keeping the spatial layers and fish occlusion intact.

Reversible implementation commits, each independently reviewable:

1. **First implementation scope: export-only vertical-slice asset preparation.** Add a deterministic exporter/recipe, a GLB containing only the approved scene subsets above, material/texture mapping and camera/glass-plane metadata, and manifest entries. Exclude the posed Blender fish and offline particles. Do not change the default runtime, host, dependencies, or approved source layout. Verify GLB opens with the vendored loader and inspect geometry/material counts.
2. Add a feature-gated live slice environment using those meshes and existing fish simulation, including coordinate mapping and bidirectional depth occlusion. Keep the plate as a toggleable comparison, not a compositing layer in the live mode.
3. Add shared camera-to-fragment RGB extinction to environment and fish, then restrained analytic in-scattering and integrated rear material/top light. Review the visual gate before more work.
4. If the gate passes, migrate the remaining approved 3D plant/substrate/wood geometry, batching material groups and optimizing only measured bottlenecks. Small spatial adjustments are permitted to improve near/mid/far separation while preserving the recognizable aquarium.
5. Add sparse motes and very slow coherent light/plant-tip movement only if needed; capture runtime visual and performance evidence. Preserve engine pause and hidden-window idle. Keep each change separately revertible and do not merge rejected experiments.

### Principal risks and checks

- **Material fidelity:** Cycles procedural/view-dependent nodes do not map directly to glTF. Compare baked or approximated green, brown, and gravel color under runtime lighting before exporting every mesh.
- **Alpha and shadows:** Dense fine foliage can cause expensive overdraw or harsh cutouts; inspect fish behind leaves and whether moving tips agree with shadow/depth passes.
- **Camera and scale:** Blender-to-Three axis conversion, FOV, far clipping, front-glass plane, and fish swim extents must be calibrated from exported bounds, not guessed from screen pixels.
- **Performance:** Full geometry, per-material draws, shadow passes, and fragment cost may miss 60 FPS. Profile the slice and scale complexity only with measured evidence; do not flatten the tank preemptively.
- **Reproducibility:** The local `.blend` is ignored by Git. The implementation must make its exported assets and generation recipe reviewable, and document source preservation without adding dependencies.
- **Art identity:** The approved scene is a bright, lush freshwater tank. The reference video's deep backdrop is a spatial cue, not authority to crush foreground blacks or wash the vegetation teal.

This report is the stop point. The next action requires the separate, export-only implementation commit above; no runtime migration has been performed here.
